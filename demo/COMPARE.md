# v1 → v2: five things you can now say on stage

Five claims **DEMO.2** can defend under a hostile question. **DEMO.1** could not defend any of
them — not because it was dishonest, but because it had no mechanism a sceptic could poke.
Each line below is followed by the objection it survives.

---

**1. "Click anything on this screen. It opens the record it came from, with the line highlighted."**
*v1:* provenance was grey type — `Source · MLS MR-4471902` — an assertion about a record nobody
could see. *v2:* the chip opens the record. The email, the listing, the note and the transaction
file are rendered on the page and in the drawer **from the same arrays**, so the demo structurally
cannot show a fact its record does not contain. → *Survives: "how do I know it didn't make that up?"*

**2. "Twenty-seven facts, twenty-seven sourced. That number is counted at render, not typed into a slide."**
*v1:* had no count and no rule; editorial lines like "the seller's timing says so" sat in the same
type as MLS data with nothing marking the difference. *v2:* `renderCounter()` counts the `FACTS`
array and prints an integrity banner; if a claim ever reaches the page without a source, the banner
turns and says so. The failure mode is visible instead of silent. → *Survives: "what happens when
it gets one wrong?"*

**3. "The system never says verified. It says sourced. Only your agent says confirmed."**
*v1:* used "grounded facts" and left the reader to guess who had stood behind them. *v2:* the
vocabulary is split and held everywhere — chips say *sourced*, the flagged blocks say *awaiting
confirmation*, the checklist is what a human clears. The liability line is drawn in the interface,
not in a disclaimer. → *Survives: "who is responsible if it's wrong?"*

**4. "His second question has no answer in our records — and here is the empty field, in the record."**
*v1:* the `[CONFIRM:]` placeholder asserted a gap. *v2:* opens listing #114, where every field is
populated and **permit status** is highlighted reading *"— not recorded in this listing."* The gap
is demonstrated. Absences get the same treatment: the *No record* chip opens a panel naming the
three sources searched and the one thing that was not — anything outside this agent's book.
→ *Survives: "isn't 'I don't know' just a hedge?"*

**5. "The insight that wins this deal came from your own note, on 12 March — not from a pool."**
*v1:* stated the Henderson insight beautifully and sourced it to nothing; a sceptic could reasonably
assume aggregated MLS or shared brokerage data. *v2:* it is chipped to `[Your note · Mar 12]`,
opens the agent's own call note, and the drawer's footer reads *"It has never left this book, and
nothing here is pooled with any other agent's."* The persistent bar says the same thing on every
screen. → *Survives: "so you're harvesting our client relationships?"* — the single most dangerous
question a brokerage will ask.

---

### What v2 deletes on purpose

Cut, because under the no-third-state rule they were assertions with no record behind them:

- **"Four doors from Coriander Lane."** The best line in v1 and entirely unsourced — neither the
  listing nor the transaction file records the distance. v2 says only that both records name
  Marlowe Ridge, and the derivation chip shows exactly that and admits what it does not establish.
- **"Sellers are motivated — their agent has said so out loud twice."** Hearsay with no note behind it.
- **"The ask is defensible; it is not a bargain. There is room."** A judgment. v2 shows the arithmetic
  — $1,423 against a $1,324–$1,360 closed band — and stops. The read is the agent's to make.
- **Copy buttons.** They exported the words and left the chips behind, which is precisely the
  property v2 is selling.

### Cost, stated plainly

v2 is less immediately dazzling. v1's briefing read like a finished answer; v2's reads like a
sourced dossier, and the wow lands a beat later because it arrives with its evidence rather than
ahead of it. That is the trade: **v1 is more impressive on first view, v2 is the only one of the
two that survives the second question.**
